Start with the result

Nobody wants an audit. They want the time and the money back.

Hours

Time returned

Re-typing, chasing, copy-pasting between tools, searching for information.

Capacity

Work you can say yes to

Demand often isn't the constraint. The team's time is.

Leaks

Money that slips out

Leads not followed up, tools nobody uses, subscriptions paid twice.

The audit is how you find exactly where those three are hiding.

What businesses are already doing

Everyone is trying AI. Most are on separate rafts.

One ChatGPT account per person

Personal accounts, personal prompts, nothing shared or governed.

Data scattered everywhere

A CRM, a project board, two chat apps, notebooks, spreadsheets. Nothing in sync.

Tools bought before a diagnosis

A chatbot or voice agent sold as the answer before anyone looked at the process.

Result: "AI doesn't work"

You can't build on data in silos. The business isn't AI-ready yet, and nobody checked.

Why this matters

Most AI projects don't fail on the technology.

80%+

of AI projects fail by some estimates, about twice the rate of IT projects without AI. Top causes: the wrong problem, and the wrong data.

95%

of generative AI pilots had no measurable effect on profit in one 2025 study. A preliminary and contested figure, but the pattern is consistent.

The common thread: nobody diagnosed the business first.

RAND, "The Root Causes of Failure for Artificial Intelligence Projects", 2024 · MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (not peer-reviewed)

Where the real opportunities are

Look at operations before marketing.

What people jump to

Cold calling bots. Automatic content. LinkedIn spam. Complex funnels.

Where the money usually is

The bottlenecks in day-to-day operations: manual capture, re-typing, hand-offs, searching, reporting, and the one person everybody waits for.

  • Where is the same information typed twice?
  • Where does work wait for one person?
  • Where do people search for something the business already knows?
What not to do with AI

Remove the mechanical work so the human part gets more room.

Don't automate a broken process

Analyse the human process first. Automation only makes a bad process faster.

Don't build on scattered data

Centralise and clean first. Otherwise every AI tool gives poor answers.

Don't buy the solution before the diagnosis

The tool should come out of the audit, not the other way round.

Keep the human where the value is

Relationships, judgment and creativity stay human. Your knowledge becomes more valuable, not less.

Who to interview

Leadership tells you what they believe. The team tells you what is.

1 · Leadership

Vision, priorities, what they think takes time.

2 · The field

Most central to most peripheral. Walk through the last real case, on screen.

3 · Everyone, lightly

A short team survey to check the patterns.

The gap between those two views is where the value of the audit is.

Confidentiality promised twice. Reporting aggregated, never "who said what".

Must-asks first

Lock the time

The steps most people miss

A diagnosis, not a sales pitch.

Interviewing the team, not only the owner

The people using the tools see the real problems.

Watching the real tools

Screen-share walkthroughs show what nobody thinks to mention.

Checking data readiness

Where the data lives, who owns it, whether it can be trusted.

Saying what not to automate

Explicit exclusions build more trust than any promise.

ROI that holds up

Count hours the client can verify.

time per task (from the field) × volume (from ops) × loaded cost (salary bands)
The floor

Hours × salary. Defensible, written in the client's own KPIs.
